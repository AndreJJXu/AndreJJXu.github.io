Publication 13 · T2I benchmark

HealBench: Evaluating Psychological Comfort in Text-to-Image Generation.

Image generation moved past semantic fidelity — but can generated images actually feel restorative? HealBench makes that question measurable.

Research manuscript Text-to-image Psychological comfort Benchmark

Junjie Xu and collaborators · East China Normal University

Text-to-image models optimize semantics, aesthetics, and preference — but whether generated images elicit positive, emotionally restorative responses went unmeasured. Drawing on cognitive appraisal theory, HealBench treats comfort as an appraisal-shaped response, organizes healing content into 19 categories and 92 subcategories across six healing functions, and scores models with a two-stage HealScore pipeline — text-image alignment, heal accuracy, image quality. Current systems still fall substantially short, especially in personalization and affective resonance.

01

The question

As image generation moved beyond "is this a correct picture" to "is this a good experience", one dimension stayed unmeasured: whether an image feels psychologically comforting. Comfort is not a low-level visual attribute — it is an affective response shaped by a viewer's appraisal of restorative and emotionally supportive cues.

The question: can that response be defined, curated, and scored with enough rigor to track generation quality against it?

02

The approach

HealBench organizes restorative visual content into 19 categories and 92 subcategories, grouped into six major classes by healing function. From an initial pool of about 100K candidates, 18,000 high-quality text pairs are curated through human-model co-annotation. Scoring uses a two-stage HealScore pipeline: evaluate text-image alignment, heal accuracy, and image quality — then refine. A 2,695-instance subjective study (feature-clustered selection) anchors the metric across 14 models; SD 3.5-L augmentation extends coverage.

03

What it shows

The benchmark's verdict on current systems is uncomfortable but useful: text-to-image and editing models still face substantial limitations in producing images users perceive as psychologically comforting — with the largest gaps in personalization and affective resonance rather than in raw semantics.

HealBench is built to be tracked against: a practical way to measure the emotional and relational qualities of generated images as models improve.

04

Claim boundary

✓ Established

  • A cognitively-grounded definition of psychological comfort for T2I
  • 19 categories × 92 subcategories across six healing functions
  • A two-stage HealScore: alignment, heal accuracy, image quality
  • Human-anchored subjective evaluation across 14 models

✗ Explicitly not claimed

  • Research manuscript — not a reviewed result
  • Comfort is measured as reported response, not clinical benefit
  • Cultural scope of healing cues follows the curated content
  • No claim that high HealScore implies therapeutic efficacy
