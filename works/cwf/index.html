Publication 06 · Survey · affect & agents

Caring Without Feeling.

When agents plan, remember, and act, warmth and simulated empathy stop being tone choices — they become control signals. This survey maps the evidence.

Preprint · arXiv 2026 Survey Affective computing AI agents & governance

Junjie Xu, Xingjiao Wu*, Zihao Zhang, Yujia Xu, Yuzhe Yang, Jin Zhu, Luwei Xiao (NUS), Wen Wu, Liang He† · ECNU · National University of Singapore

Warmth, confidence, apology, validation, simulated empathy — in agentic systems these shape when people trust, delegate, verify, correct, or disengage. The survey integrates affective computing, trust in automation, human-AI interaction, and governance into one account: what generates affective behavior, how affective cues alter reliance and oversight, where those pathways become risky, and what measurement and governance can hold the line — with every claim graded by evidence strength and directness.

01

The question

Classic HCI treated software as an instrument: people formulate goals, interpret context, bear responsibility. LLM-based agents break that division — they plan, remember, call tools, and act within delegated scopes. In that setting, affective expression is not a tone choice: it coordinates capability, uncertainty, and responsibility between human and agent.

The survey asks what actually changes once systems have memory, tool use, and partial autonomy — and where the evidence actually supports strong claims.

02

The approach

The account is built in four layers. Computational mechanisms that generate affective behavior. Collaboration pathways through which affective cues alter reliance, oversight, and correction. Deployment configurations where those pathways turn into a risk cascade — over-trust, accountability displacement, verification decay, delegation creep. And measurement and governance tools for calibrated affective alignment.

Methodologically, the survey is strict: it distinguishes affective expression, perceived agent affect, and genuine emotion — attributing to current agents only the first two — and grades every claim by evidence strength and directness to agentic systems.

03

What it shows

Affect functions as a coordination layer: warmth and confidence negotiate capability and responsibility, for better and for worse. The risk cascade is concrete — uncalibrated affective cues disrupt mechanism-level calibration, which degrades collaboration quality.

The most useful finding may be the negative one: most trajectory risks discussed in the literature lack agent-specific longitudinal tests. The survey closes with a falsifiable research agenda — minimum study designs that would make each open claim sharper or weaker.

04

Claim boundary

✓ Established

  • A three-construct distinction: expression, perceived affect, genuine emotion
  • An integrated mechanisms-pathways-risks-governance account
  • Evidence-graded claims with causal language matched to study design
  • A falsifiable agenda of minimum study designs for open questions

✗ Explicitly not claimed

  • No claim that current agents have genuine emotion or subjective feeling
  • Survey synthesis, not new experiments
  • Longitudinal agent-specific evidence is explicitly marked missing
  • No prescription of specific products or deployment decisions
