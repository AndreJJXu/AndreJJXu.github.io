Publication 09 · Audio-based image generation

Phoenix: Prioritize the Hierarchical Observation of Auditory Expression.

The audio→text→image shortcut drops what captions never carried. Phoenix keeps the auditory expression in the loop.

Under review · IEEE TCSVT Audio-based image generation Two-stage design Aesthetic guidance

Junjie Xu, Xingjiao Wu*, Zihao Zhang, Luwei Xiao, Daoguo Dong, Liang He · East China Normal University

Captioning audio and feeding the text to a text-to-image model looks like a free lunch — until cumulative stage errors and information loss at the text bottleneck surface as semantic misalignment. Phoenix is a two-stage design that never reduces audio to a single sentence: an essence-clip-aware audio block keeps the informative segments, multi-dimensional captioning preserves layered content, and mutual-information guidance with impression and aesthetic perspectives steers generation.

2benchmarks: VGGSound and VEGAS

01

The question

With text-to-image models this good, why generate from audio directly? Because the popular shortcut — caption the audio, then generate from the caption — pays twice: errors accumulate across stages, and the text medium systematically drops auditory information no caption was asked to carry. End-to-end alternatives keep semantics but struggle with cost, stability, and generalization.

The question: can a two-stage design keep the strengths of pretrained generators while refusing the lossy bottleneck?

02

The approach

Phoenix's two stages are shaped by the failure modes. Stage one, an essence-clip-aware audio block, identifies and preserves the informative clips of the audio rather than averaging them away; multi-dimensional captioning then describes the audio along several layers instead of one sentence. Stage two generates the image under mutual-information guidance with impression and aesthetic perspectives, keeping the output faithful to the auditory expression.

03

What it shows

On VGGSound and VEGAS, Phoenix maintains strong semantic alignment with the input audio while reaching high-quality visual outputs — outperforming text-bottleneck baselines precisely where they fail: classes whose identity lives in temporal structure rather than in captionable content.

Qualitative results show sound classes like church bells and elk bugling rendered with the right visual identity — where one-sentence captions drift to generic scenes.

04

Claim boundary

✓ Established

  • An essence-clip-aware audio block preserving informative segments
  • Multi-dimensional captioning instead of a single-sentence bottleneck
  • Mutual-information guidance with impression and aesthetic perspectives
  • Stronger semantic alignment and image quality on VGGSound and VEGAS

✗ Explicitly not claimed

  • Under review — results preliminary until acceptance
  • Benchmark scope: environmental-sound datasets, not music
  • No claim of real-time generation
  • Aesthetic guidance shapes style, not ground-truth appearance
