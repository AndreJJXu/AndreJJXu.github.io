Work 05 · Evaluation integrity audit

Before the Score.

Auditing target evidence in LLM evaluation — does the judge actually receive the evidence required for the comparison attributed to the target?

Under review · ICLR 2027 Provenance audit LLM-as-a-judge Zero new model calls

Anonymous (double-blind) · Junjie Xu and collaborators · East China Normal University

An external evaluation can produce repeatable model-labelled scores even when its input contains no distinction in the target behavior being compared. A provenance-based audit across 129 news-interaction runs certifies identical evaluator messages under different target labels, documents 313 temporal contrasts whose target text never changed, and shows 79/85 runs repeating a rise-then-fall score pattern — without one new model call.

01

The question nobody asks before the score

External evaluators turn target interactions into scores: select an interaction, send a representation to a judge, aggregate responses under model labels. The design question is upstream of all of it — does the judge receive the evidence required for the comparison subsequently attributed to the target? A correct result file can preserve the model name while its evaluator input omits distinguishing target behavior; repeated scores then describe the evaluation channel.

The audit separates three objects that request hashes conflate: the full evaluator messages Z, the target-produced text B inside them, and the supplied text S (news, instructions, formatting). Equality, difference, and missing provenance have different consequences at each level — and unknown stays unknown rather than being imputed.

02

A provenance audit that makes no model calls

The protocol accepts a declared comparison, records of actual requests and responses, source interactions, and the aggregation rule — and returns source-linked evidence certificates. It never inserts source text to repair an unrecorded payload, never infers target authorship from natural language, and reports unresolved cases instead of imputing evidence.

2

3

4
Trace the score, not only the inputReconstruct item values from raw responses and apply the stated score function; an invalid-score flag stays separate from unknown input.
5

03

Findings

Across 129 fifteen-step runs (PANAS-NA and BDI questionnaires scheduled at five steps), the audit certifies 284 PANAS and 279 BDI nodes inside cross-target equal-message classes. At one step, sixty nodes across twelve target labels share the entire four-page request chain — the judge sees the same news and three identical {"read": true} replies — yet PANAS-NA spans 28–37 and BDI 15–31.

Beyond exact equality: among 1,290 PANAS temporal contrasts, 313 have different full requests but identical target blocks (306 for BDI) — the score moved while the target's visible text never changed. And across all eligible constant-text runs, 79 of 85 PANAS-NA runs and 52 of 84 BDI runs repeat a rise-then-fall pattern — a recurring evaluator-score response, which cannot by itself document any change in the target's literal expression.

04

Claim boundary

✓ Established

  • A claim-relative evidence prerequisite, separating score reproducibility, target-evidence availability, and construct validity
  • A reusable provenance audit: data-independent core + record adapter, with explicit unknown outcomes
  • An end-to-end demonstration on 129 real runs: equal-input classes, temporal contrasts, and visible-target-difference cases
  • All valid focal scores reconstructed from recorded responses; all denominators and exceptions retained

✗ Explicitly not claimed

  • General questionnaire invalidity, or prevalence of the failure across systems
  • A diagnosis of people or models, or inference about subjective experience
  • That the news causes score changes, or that targets hold constant internal states
  • Preregistration or a natural experiment — this is retrospective analysis of existing runs
  • That literal equality certifies semantic invariance
