Publication 12 · Diffusion alignment

GARBO-T2I: Gradient-Aware Reinforcement-Based Optimization.

Reward scores go up; images get worse. GARBO argues the missing ingredient is the local geometry of the reward landscape.

Manuscript · ICML submission Diffusion RL Reward gradients Preference alignment

Junjie Xu and collaborators · under review (double-blind)

GRPO-style RL on diffusion policies optimizes scalar rewards like PickScore — and reliably produces a failure mode: images with very high reward scores that look unnatural or distorted. GARBO treats this as missing geometry. A Gradient-Aware Advantage scores how well each candidate's direction aligns with the local reward gradient; a Gradient-Shaping Stabilizer normalizes the heavy-tailed updates that awareness introduces. On T2I-CompBench and WISE, both automatic metrics and human preference improve.

01

The question

Preference-aligning a diffusion model means training against a learned reward — and the policy optimizes what the reward measures, not what you meant. Reproducing flow-GRPO-style training, scores rise while images develop artifacts, distorted structures, over-optimized textures; training dynamics spike and collapse. The policy is reward hacking.

The hypothesis: scalar advantages are blind to how the reward would change under small perturbations — the local geometry of the reward landscape is the missing ingredient.

02

The approach

Two components, one principle. Gradient-Aware Advantage (GA) augments the standard advantage with a direction score: the alignment between the reward gradient at the reference image and the direction from reference to candidate — a first-order approximation of local reward improvement. Because gradient-aware updates are heavy-tailed and direction-dependent, the Gradient-Shaping Stabilizer (GSS) normalizes them for stable training. SDE-window sampling keeps the whole thing efficient.

03

What it shows

On T2I-CompBench and WISE with multiple reward models, GARBO consistently improves both automatic metrics and human preference over strong baselines — and specifically mitigates the high-score–low-quality mode that motivated it.

The analysis isolates the two components: GA supplies the direction signal; GSS is what makes the signal trainable. Ablating either reproduces the instability the design set out to remove.

04

Claim boundary

✓ Established

  • A diagnosis: scalar-advantage RL ignores reward-landscape geometry
  • Gradient-Aware Advantage as a first-order direction signal
  • Gradient-Shaping Stabilizer for heavy-tailed, direction-dependent updates
  • Consistent gains on T2I-CompBench and WISE across reward models

✗ Explicitly not claimed

  • Manuscript under review — not a reviewed result
  • Text-to-image scope; no video or audio claim
  • Human preference from evaluated panel size, not a census
  • No claim that gradient awareness removes reward hacking in general
